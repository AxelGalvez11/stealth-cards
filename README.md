# Lucida

The flashcard database any AI can plug into. FSRS scheduling, clean flip cards, web + iOS.

## Design and web app

The design canvas is the source of truth for how the app looks: https://claude.ai/artifact/VLXyuTGdroHdrJ2qNAmiGs

- `design/` makes every canvas board (`node design/build.mjs`, checked by `node design/check.mjs`).
- The boards keep what they all share once, in three files beside them in `design/canvas/project` (`lucida-logic.js`, `lucida-themes.js`, `lucida.css`; `design/slim.mjs`), so a board holds only its own markup, props and logic. Publish those three files with the boards. A script that reads a board reads it with `readBoard()` (`design/slim.mjs`), which puts the shared code back.
- `design/to-web.mjs` turns those boards into the web app's screens, so the app matches the canvas.
- **Motion** has one set of timings, `design/motion.mjs` (how far a menu slides, how long a pop-up, sheet, switch and fade take, one ease-out curve, nothing bounces): the boards' CSS, `web/motion-timings.js` (made by `design/to-web.mjs`, read by `web/motion.js` for the segmented control's sliding pill and the deck cover's parallax where a browser can't do it in CSS) and the iPhone app's `Generated.motion` (`design/to-ios.mjs`) all come from it. Everything turns off with Reduce Motion (`prefers-reduced-motion`). On the web, deck, folder and class tiles also lift 4 px under the pointer, with a bigger shadow, in 0.2 s (`TILE_HOVER` in `design/motion.mjs`, which the iPhone app never reads: a phone has no pointer).
- **Sharing a link** (`design/share.mjs`, for the boards' logic): on a phone or tablet and in Safari the web app opens the browser's own share sheet (`navigator.share`), and its buttons say "Share link"; on a computer's Chrome, Edge or Firefox the link is copied and the page says "Link copied", as it always did. Closing the sheet does nothing.
- **Explain** (the AI's explanation of a card's answer, `explainButton` / `explainPanel` and `learnExplain` in `design/build.mjs`): once a card is turned over or a Learn question is answered, Explain opens the explanation without covering anything. On a computer it is a panel (360 px) to the RIGHT of the card or the question, top-aligned with it, the two sitting side by side as one centered group; a narrower window narrows the card down to 560 px, and below that (and in the web app's phone layout) the panel goes UNDER the card, or under the answers in Learn. Only a short fade, none with reduced motion. The boards are `WebReviewExplain` and `PhoneReviewExplain` (Learn's boards take `explainOpen` with `answered`). `ios/tools/explain-stub.mjs` is a stand-in for the AI that tests use (the server's `OPENROUTER_BASE`).
- `web/` is the web app. Run it with `npm run dev` and open http://localhost:3000 (no packages to install).
- http://localhost:3000/b lists every board, including empty states and dark mode.
- **Settings is a page of its own, like ChatGPT's**, with the same seven sections on the web and the iPhone, in this order: Account (profile, picture, password, Sign out, Delete account), Plan, Studying (goal, Flip animation, ...), Appearance (themes, look), Connect AI, Privacy (blocked people, your data), Help & legal. On the web `/settings` is Account and `/settings/<section>` (`account`, `plan`, `studying`, `appearance`, `connect-ai`, `privacy`, `help`) the others, so Back and links work. **On a computer, Settings' own sidebar replaces the app's** in the same place, with the same width and style: a Back row on top (to the page you came from, or Today when Settings was opened directly; `web/app.js` keeps that page in `sessionStorage` for the tab, so a reload keeps it) and the seven sections below, the current one lit like the app sidebar lights its place. It is always open, never the rail; the app sidebar's open or collapsed choice is kept and is there again after Settings; the swap is instant. The page body holds only the section's title and its rows in rounded cards (a title, a short line only where it isn't clear, the control on the right). Theme (`/settings/theme`, and a theme's own page `/settings/theme/<key>`) and Connect AI (`/connect`) are pages inside Settings, so they wear Settings' sidebar too, with their section lit (Appearance, Connect AI), also on a direct link; its Back row works as above, and the pages' own back links land on the section they came from (Appearance, Connect AI; a theme's page goes up to Theme). On the canvas `ThemePicker` (and its Free and dark boards), `WebTheme`, each theme's own board and `WebConnect` wear it, and their section links open the Settings board. A narrow screen (under 761 px) gets the iPhone board in other states: `/settings` is the list of sections, and each section a page with a back arrow. The iPhone app keeps its native list, its rows grouped into the same sections (`ios/Lucida/Screens/Settings.swift`). On the canvas: `WebSettings` has a `section` Tweak (the other Settings boards show the one they name; its Back goes to the Today board) and `PhoneSettings` one too (`All`, the iPhone's list, the default; `List` and the section names are the narrow web's pages). A computer with nobody signed in has no Plan section. Help & legal opens Help, the Terms and the Privacy Policy on lucida.cards.
- **The web sidebar collapses** to a 78 px rail of icons (the small button at its top, "Collapse sidebar", which becomes "Expand sidebar"; at most a .2 s width change, none with Reduce Motion). Each icon keeps its name as its tooltip (`title`) and its accessible name, the page takes the freed width, and the choice is kept on that device in `localStorage` (`lucida.sidebar`; if storage throws it starts open). `web/side.js` is the state, `sidebar` in `design/build.mjs` the markup and its CSS (`.sc-side`), and the canvas's Main board has a `collapsed` Tweak (any board with the sidebar follows it through the sample data's `chrome`). Phones have no sidebar.

## Online (Vercel + Supabase)

- Vercel builds the pages from the canvas boards (`vercel.json`), and `api/index.js` answers `/api`, `/auth`, `/mcp`, and `/media` with the same code as the local server (`web/handler.mjs`).
- **Sign-in** (`web/auth.mjs`, Supabase Auth): a 6-digit code by email, or Google and Apple through Supabase. The session lives in two cookies the page's scripts can't read, and renews itself before it runs out. Signed out, the app shows only the sign-in pages.
- **Each person has their own library**: one row in the `libraries` table (keyed by their user id), and their pictures and sound in their own folder of the private `media` bucket (`web/supa.mjs`). Each request works on its own copy (`withLibrary` in `web/store.mjs`), so people never see each other's cards.
- **Personal MCP links**: AI apps connect at `https://app.lucida.cards/mcp/lk_…`, shown on the Connect AI page. The link opens only that person's library; "Make a new link" there turns off the old one. (Plain `/mcp` works only on your own computer.)
- **Sign-in for AI apps** (`web/oauth.mjs`, `supabase/oauth.sql`): Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini and other apps add `https://app.lucida.cards/mcp`, the person signs in to Lucida and presses Allow (the app's own page, the ConnectConsent board), and the app gets tokens (OAuth 2.1: PKCE S256, refresh tokens that work once each, apps identified by a registered `client_id` or by an https address, CIMD). `/mcp` takes that token (`Authorization: Bearer …`) or the old secret link; with neither it answers 401 and points at `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource`. Only hashes of tokens are kept. Settings → Connect AI lists the apps a person allowed, with Disconnect. The address the documents name is `https://app.lucida.cards` (`LUCIDA_ORIGIN` sets another); this computer doesn't ask apps to sign in unless `LUCIDA_MCP_LOGIN=1`. Run `supabase/oauth.sql` before deploying (it only adds four tables). A code lasts 5 minutes, an access token 1 hour, a refresh token 90 days; `LUCIDA_CODE_SECS`, `LUCIDA_ACCESS_SECS` and `LUCIDA_REFRESH_SECS` shorten them for checks.
- **Passwords** are optional: Settings → Account → Password sets one (Supabase's own), and sign-in has "Use a password". Directory reviewers, who can't get an email code, sign in this way.
- **Directories**: `server.json` is for the official MCP Registry (`mcp-publisher`; the domain proof is a file at `https://lucida.cards/.well-known/mcp-registry-auth`). The page people read is `lucida.cards/connect` (a board, `SiteConnect`, made into `web/connect-guide.html` by `design/to-site.mjs`; the app's own `/connect` is the Connect AI page). `samples/reviewer-library.txt` is four decks for the reviewer account: import it from the Welcome page (Bring your cards → Anki).
- Vercel gets `SUPABASE_URL` and the keys from the Supabase integration. Without them, the app saves to `data/` on your computer and nobody signs in.
- **Landing page**: the Landing boards on the canvas become `web/landing.html` (`node design/to-site.mjs`), shown at lucida.cards. Its links open the app at app.lucida.cards. The Privacy and Terms boards become lucida.cards/privacy and /terms (their words are in `design/legal.mjs`).
- **Gradient pictures**: the landing page and the sign-in wall move dozens of gradient cards, which phones can't redraw live, so there each card's colors come from a small picture in `web/art/` under one grain tile (`web/fast.css`). `node design/art.mjs` draws them with Chrome; run it after changing the wall cards (`design/wall.mjs`), the palettes, or the generator. The canvas still draws them live.

Supabase settings sign-in needs (Supabase dashboard → Authentication), all set:
- Emails → SMTP Settings: Resend (connected from Resend → Settings → Integrations → Supabase), sending as Lucida, team@lucida.cards. Supabase's own sender only mails the project's team, and templates can't be edited without this.
- Emails → "Magic link or OTP" and "Confirm sign up": the code in the email with `{{ .Token }}`.
- Sign In / Providers → Email: Email OTP Length 6 (the sign-in page has six boxes).
- URL Configuration: Site URL `https://app.lucida.cards`, and Redirect URLs `https://app.lucida.cards/**`, `https://lucida-eight.vercel.app/**`, `http://localhost:3000/**`.
- Sign In / Providers: turn on Google and Apple with their own client ids (Google Cloud and Apple Developer). Until then, those buttons say they aren't set up yet.

## The site's pages, for search engines and AI answers (lucida.cards)

- Every page of lucida.cards besides the app is static HTML made from canvas boards: the landing page, Pricing, Privacy, Terms and Connect from their own boards, and every other page from **one JSON file in `design/site/`** (comparisons at `vs/…` and `…-alternative`, the hubs `blog`, `compare` and `features`, features at `features/…`, who it's for at `for/…`, and `faq`). `design/site.mjs` says what a file holds, reads them, and sorts them into the blog's categories (Comparisons, Features, Guides, Help). Three boards draw them, each with a phone twin and a page picker (SiteCompare, SiteFeature, SiteFaq), and SiteOg draws each page's picture. A page is one markup that fits any width, with one h1, real tables and lists, breadcrumbs, and its sources with the day each was checked.
- **One header on every page** of lucida.cards (the landing page, Pricing, Privacy, Terms, Connect, the blog, the hubs, the articles, the FAQ and the 404, and the canvas boards that draw them): the logo, then Blog, Compare, Features, Pricing and Sign in, then Get started. Under 761 px the five links sit behind one menu button (a `<details>`: it opens at once and needs no script) and Get started stays beside it. It is one function in `design/build.mjs` (`siteHeader`, `HEAD_CSS`, `HEAD_METHOD`, which says where each link goes on the site and on the canvas); the landing page's own sections (How it works, Card types) are links in its footer.
- A page reads like an article (after higgsfield.ai/blog): one left-aligned column (680 px of words, 16 px on a phone and 17 px on a computer) that is **centered on the page at every width**, breadcrumb pills that start at Blog (Blog › Comparisons › the page, with matching BreadcrumbList data), a big title, chips (Lucida, the day it was updated, minutes to read), the lead, then the page's own picture as the hero. "On this page" is a tree (a section's numbered steps, bold lead-ins and, on the FAQ, its questions are under it): from 1,280 px it stands in the left margin and sticks (the page is three tracks, a margin each side and the column between, so the tree never moves the column; a few lines of script in `design/to-site.mjs` mark the section being read), and narrower it is one closed row under the lead. Nothing breaks out of the column. **Tables** keep their real columns on every screen and sit in the column; one wider than its box scrolls sideways inside it (a focusable region, with a soft shade on the edge that has more), and the page never scrolls sideways; past six rows the rest wait behind "Show all N". Questions are one rounded card of closed rows (+ / −); a card with the page's title and "Start free" (its gradient drifts slowly, as the app's Today card does); at most three related pages as picture cards; the sources in one closed row, last. **A hub** (`/compare`, `/features`) is one container as wide as the blog's: its crumbs, title, lead, featured page, cards, table, headings and lists start at one left edge, with the blog's gaps. **The blog** (`lucida.cards/blog`, the hub board's `blog` page) is a front page with a headline, tabs, a search box (a few lines of script; without script every card shows), a featured page and a grid of cards for each category. A card shows its picture, title and "7 min · Sep 30, 2026", with no category pill.
- **Motion** (only what the owner asked for, super subtle, `SKY_CSS`): the clouds in the sky at the top of every page drift 16% of their own width to one side and back (72 to 120 s each way, eased, so the loop has no jump; the night sky too), and the gradient that ends a page (the Start free card, the landing page's closing band) drifts like the app's Today card (`scDrift`, 16 s back and forth); the grain over it stays still. Transforms only, and `prefers-reduced-motion` stops both. The pictures never move.
- **Dark mode.** The text pages follow the system (`prefers-color-scheme`): the colors are CSS variables with a light set and a dark set (`design/scheme.mjs`, which must give the same values as the app's `theme()`; `check-site` compares them), so one markup serves both; on the canvas the boards' Dark switch shows the same two looks. The landing page and Pricing follow too.
- **Pictures.** Each page's picture is a bold full-bleed gradient (13 styles in 24 palettes, after Awwwards' "Trendy Gradients in Web Design") with a scene of the app's own flashcards on it; `design/site.mjs` (`PICTURES`, `pictureOf`) says which, and a page file can set its own with a `picture` key (`scene`, `style`, `grad`, `other`). **Every page's gradient is its own**: no style and palette pair is used twice, and the pictures must look different, which a check measures: `design/og.mjs` also draws each gradient alone (no words, no veil, no scene) as a grid of 32 × 17 colors into `design/og-bg.json`, and `design/check-site.mjs` compares them in CIELAB (mean ΔE of the cells): every two pictures at least 30 apart, and pictures that sit next to each other on the blog, a hub or in Keep reading at least 60 apart. A card in a scene (`ogFace`) is the Review screen's card scaled to the width it has here (radius, padding, line, left-aligned words, a faint note, a chip in the corner); Lucida's wears a deck cover (the gradient of the app's covers) in the corner where the app has its chip. A picture carries no web address and no category word (the pictures' words are in `web/og/manifest.json`, and a check reads them). It looks the same on a light screen and a dark one. `design/og.mjs` draws one PNG per page into `web/og/` (with a light grain baked in: it is also the link preview, `og:image`), and on the site and in the boards the app's film grain (`.sc-grain`, `web/fast.css`; on the canvas `GRAIN_LAYER`) is laid over each picture, so it reads like a deck cover. No photos, no AI pictures, no other apps' logos.
- **Diagrams, tables, the app's screens and "Test yourself" in the articles.** `design/site/_visuals.json` says, for each article page, which drawings go in which section (`figs`: `at` is the section's h2), which sections' bold-lead-in bullets become a table (`tables`), and its 3 or 4 test cards (`test`). `design/visuals.mjs` holds the kinds of drawing, the table of the app's screens (`SCREENS`: which board, which crop) and the code that places them; `design/build.mjs` draws each kind in HTML and CSS (inline SVG for lines), so the canvas and the site show the same thing in light and dark. The words stay the page file's: a diagram holds only labels and captions that restate the page; a "Pick X if …" card, a checklist row, a fork and a table row hold a bullet's own words; the test cards say only what the page says (`check-site` checks all of that). The app's real screens are pictures of the app's own boards: `node design/screens.mjs` draws `web/shots/<screen>[-phone][-dark].webp` with Chrome and the app's local server (it redraws only what an app board or a crop changed). On the site a `<picture>` gives a phone the phone's board and a dark system the dark one; on the canvas the same board is a `dc-import`, cropped the same way. **The flashcards in the articles are the app's card** (`.sp-fc`): the Review screen's card (radius 36, padding 56 × 44, a 1 px line, the app's shadow, left-aligned words in weight 500 centered in the card, a faint note and a chip in the corner), with every measure in em from the size of its words so one card is the other at another size (a phone gets PhoneReview's measures), in the app's dark card too (#141414, #262626 line, no shadow). The pick, example and word cards, the Learn question and the small cards in the other drawings use it. A "Test yourself" card is a `<details>` with the question on its front and the answer on its back, so a tap turns it on the same card, at once, and a tap again turns it back (the chip in the corner says what a tap does, and the faint line under the answer is the question); on a phone the cards are one row that scrolls sideways, on a computer two across.
- `design/site/_llms.json` holds the intro, a line for each page and quick facts for `lucida.cards/llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt`. `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt` (every AI crawler named), the structured data (JSON-LD), the titles and the link previews all come from the same page files (`design/seo.mjs`), so they can't go stale.
- **Tab icons.** Every page of both hosts (every page of lucida.cards, the app, its public decks and profiles, sign-in and the 404) names the same four icons in its `<head>` (`ICON_LINKS` in `design/seo.mjs`; `web/app.html` and the app's sign-in page in `web/handler.mjs` carry the same lines): `/favicon.ico` (`sizes="32x32"`, which makes Chrome and Edge take the SVG over it; Safari on a Mac, which has no SVG tab icons, takes the .ico), `/icon.svg` (the tab icon: three white dots on a black rounded tile, with a faint edge on a dark tab strip), `/icons/icon-192.png` and `/apple-touch-icon.png` (the home-screen icon: black dots on white). `web/favicon.ico` (16, 32 and 48 pixels in one file, PNGs inside; `design/ico.mjs` writes and reads it) and the two root copies `web/apple-touch-icon.png` and `web/apple-touch-icon-precomposed.png` (where iPhones look when a page names none) are made by `design/og.mjs` along with `web/icons/*`, so nothing is a hand-made file and the same icon always gives the same bytes; `node design/og.mjs --icons` draws just them. `vercel.json` gives each its own type and a day of cache; as the files are in `web/`, both hosts hand them out at the root. No manifest: Android Chrome shows the tab icon from these links and makes a home-screen icon from the 192-pixel one. `check-site` checks the links on every page, each file's type and size, the routes and headers on both hosts and that no robots.txt blocks them; `check-pages --icons` opens every page (and the app's, through its own server) in Chrome, which asks for the icons by itself, and fails on any 404.
- After changing a page file: `npm run site` (needs Chrome and an internet connection for the fonts; it takes about ten minutes). It rebuilds the boards, measures how tall each needs to be (`design/site-measure.mjs`, into `design/site-heights.json`), writes the site, draws the pictures into `web/og/` (`design/og.mjs`, also the icons in `web/icons/`) and runs the checks, including every page in headless Chrome (`design/check-pages.mjs`). Commit what it changes. Vercel runs only `design/to-site.mjs`, which stops with a message if a page file changed after the boards were built.
- `node design/check-site.mjs` builds the site and checks every page (title and description length, one h1, canonical, link preview, structured data against schema.org, links, the sitemap, robots.txt, llms files, the page files, both hosts' routing). `--strict` makes a link to a page that doesn't exist yet a failure, `--online` also asks every outside link (sources) whether it still answers, `--no-build` skips the build. `node design/check-pages.mjs` opens every page as a phone (390) and as a computer (1440), light and dark: no sideways scrolling, pictures load (each under the app's grain), the diagrams fit (none over 440 px tall on a phone) and the screens are the phone's on a phone and the dark ones in the dark, text contrast of 4.5 to 1 or more, reading text 16 px or more, the same header on every page and its menu button on a phone, the article column centered (margins equal within 1 px), tables that keep their columns and scroll inside their box, a hub's title, headings, cards, table and lists on one left edge, every flashcard the app's card, and the pages' own controls (a question opens and closes, a test card turns to its answer on the same card, "Show all", "On this page", the blog's tabs and search); it ends by checking the motion (only the clouds and the closing gradient move, and not with reduced motion; the layout never moves). `--quick` checks a few pages.
- `vercel.json`: on the `lucida.cards` host, the app's own paths (`/sign-in`, `/@name`, `/d/…`, `/join/…`, `/api/…` and the rest) still go to app.lucida.cards; `/`, `/pricing`, `/privacy` and `/terms` are their pages; `/vs/…`, `/features/…`, `/for/…`, `/compare`, `/faq` and `/<name>-alternative` are files in `web/site/`; `/robots.txt`, `/sitemap.xml`, `/llms.txt` and `/llms-full.txt` are the site's own (the app's robots.txt is `web/robots-app.txt`); anything else is a 404 (`web/404.html`). `design/vercel-routes.mjs` reads vercel.json the way Vercel does, so the checks can test all that without deploying.
- The public deck and profile pages of the app carry JSON-LD too (`web/jsonld.mjs`), and the app's sitemap dates every profile and deck.

## Live (play with friends)

- From a deck on a computer, **Play live** opens a room (`/live/<code>`): the big screen shows a 6-digit code and a QR code, friends join on their phones at lucida.cards/join (or `/join/<code>` from the QR code) with a name and no account, and everyone answers the same questions, made from the deck's cards the way Learn mode makes them (or the questions the person's AI wrote). Points for right and fast answers, a leaderboard between questions, a podium, and the final leaderboard on each phone.
- The host's page runs the game (`web/live.js`): the questions, the time, and the points. It's kept in that tab, so a reload picks up where it was. Phones only send what was tapped; a phone that reloads, or the same name on another phone, gets its points back.
- Online, the messages go straight between the browsers over Supabase Realtime (public broadcast channels, with the project's public key), and each room's code is a row in the `live_rooms` table (`supabase/live_rooms.sql`) while it can be joined. On your computer the rooms stay in memory and the local server passes the messages along (`web/rooms.mjs`). `LUCIDA_LIVE_REALTIME=<Supabase project URL>` tries Realtime from your computer instead.

## Practice test

- **Practice test** sits beside Flashcards and Learn on a deck's page, and on a folder's page (all its decks, not the paused ones). The set-up picks 10, 20, 30 or all the questions, the kinds (multiple choice, true or false, written, matching, fill in the blank) and a time limit (off, 10, 20 or 30 minutes), and says once that tests don't change the review schedule. The questions are Learn mode's (`choiceQuestion` in `web/db.js`, the same builders on the iPhone in `LearnEngine.swift`) plus the ones an AI wrote with `add_quiz`; each question has a card of its own, a matching question is four or five pairs, and the kinds are sections in the order multiple choice, true or false, fill in the blank, matching, written.
- Taking it is calm: numbered ("12 of 20"), back and forward, a flag, a list of all the questions, a quiet clock when there's a time limit (it counts only while the page shows, and submits the test when it runs out), and nothing about right or wrong until Submit, which asks first when some are unanswered. Choices and matching are exact (a matching question is right only when every pair is); a written answer gets Learn's spelling check (case, accents, small typos), and **Count it as right** on the results fixes one it missed. The results show the percent, "16 of 20", the time, every question with your answer and the right one, Explain where the card has it, **Retake the ones I missed** (a new test of just those questions) and **Study the missed cards now** (a normal review of those cards: only that review moves the schedule). A test itself logs no review.
- The test in progress is kept in the browser (`localStorage` key `lucida.test`) or on the phone (`UserDefaults`), so a reload or a restart opens it where it was. A finished test is saved with the library (`tests`; the `test.save` and `test.fix` actions in `web/store.mjs`): the 200 newest results, with every question kept for the newest 10, listed on the deck page as a few dates and scores, and read by AI apps through the read-only `get_test_results` tool (`web/mcp.mjs`).
- The boards are `WebTest` and `PhoneTest` (`design/test-boards.mjs`: set up, each kind of question, Submit and Leave, results, results with only the missed), and the deck page's button and past results are states of `WebDeck` and `PhoneDeck` (a deck with tests taken), the folder page's button and last-test line of `WebDecks` and `PhoneLibrary` (which `WebLibraryFolder` and `PhoneLibraryFolder` show).

## Questions Lucida writes for Learn mode

- Learn mode asks questions made from the cards (`web/db.js`, and `ios/Lucida/Data/LearnEngine.swift` on the iPhone) and the questions an AI app wrote with `add_quiz`. With the owner's OpenRouter key (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`; `OPENROUTER_BASE` points a test at a stand-in, `ios/tools/stub-openrouter.mjs`), Lucida's own AI writes them too (`web/quizai.mjs`, `POST /api/quiz`): when Learn mode starts and the next cards have no question yet it asks for 20 at once, and again about 5 questions from the end. One question for each card, of the type that fits it (multiple choice with 3 wrong answers from the same subject, true or false, or fill in the blank, each with a one-line why), written from only the card's front, back and note, in a JSON schema, and checked (the right answer is there, the wrong ones are 3 different ones that aren't it, a blank is one blank that doesn't give the answer away); a question that fails the checks is dropped, and that card isn't asked about again until it changes.
- They are saved on the card (`saveQuiz` in `web/store.mjs`) in the shape of `add_quiz` questions, with `by: "Lucida"` (a fill-in-the-blank one is `kind: "blank"`), so a card is paid for once and Learn mode and the practice test reuse the question. A card that already has questions (an AI app's too) is never asked about, and editing a card's front, back or text clears them. The model settings are Explain's (V4.1 Flash then V4 Flash, only hosts that keep nothing and run 8-bit or better, the quickest first, no thinking, plain V4 Flash when nothing usable comes back) plus `require_parameters`, since there is a schema.
- Each request is one batch of the day: Free gets `FREE_QUIZ_BATCHES` (3), Pro `PRO_QUIZ_BATCHES` (30), in `web/plans.mjs`; a batch that gives nothing is given back, and a request with nothing to write uses none. Nobody waits: until the questions arrive Learn mode asks with its builders, and when the AI isn't set up or the day's batches are used (503, 402) it quietly goes on with them. The privacy policy says the card's text goes to OpenRouter for quiz questions, as it does for Explain (`design/legal.mjs`).

## Making cards, a deck's Guide, and its Sources

Run `supabase/materials.sql` on the database before deploying (it adds one column, `shared_decks.guide`, so a shared deck can show its Guide).

- **Make cards** (`web/make.mjs` on the server, `web/make.js` in the page, the WebMake and PhoneMake boards): from a PDF, a PowerPoint, a Word file, caption files (.srt, .vtt), pictures, a recording (an audio file or the microphone), a YouTube link, pasted text, or a topic. A make runs as small requests the page drives (`upload`, `start`, one `step` for each part, `plan` for recordings and videos, `finish`, `save`, `cancel`, and `job` to pick up where it was), so a long source never meets Vercel's 60 second or 4.5 MB limits. Text is pulled out without an AI (`web/extract.mjs`: PDF, pptx, docx, text, and how long an audio file is; `web/captions.mjs`: captions, with the cues' words joined into units a minute long labelled with their start time, the lines an automatic caption says twice dropped); only pictures, scanned pages, recordings and videos go to a model that can see or hear. The person checks the cards (edit, remove, keep) before they are saved, and they are marked as made by Lucida with the page or time they came from.
- **Long recordings** (`web/audiosplit.js`, used by the page before anything is sent): the speech service takes 25 MB and gives up after a minute of working, so a recording over 18 MB or ten minutes is cut into even parts, each a whole file of its own, without decoding or re-encoding anything (between frames for MP3 and AAC, between pages for Ogg Opus and Vorbis, a new header for WAV and for M4A), reading the file a megabyte at a time. Two hours of MP3 at 64 kbps is 13 parts. The parts go up in order as one make and the server adds each part's start to its words' times. FLAC, WebM, a video and an MP4 in fragments can't be cut and say so in plain words when over 25 MB; the microphone starts a new file every ten minutes. The iPhone app cuts with AVFoundation instead.
- **Starter notes and audio cards.** Every part's AI call also returns a short overview and 3 to 6 notes (a heading, where in the material, a few lines with key terms in bold, a table where the material has one), so there is no extra pass; `finish` joins them in order under the source's name (cut evenly if a long book gives more than a Guide may hold) and the review step shows them beside the cards. `save` puts them in the deck: as its Guide if it has none, else as a new page named for the source, never over what is written (`notes: 'guide' | 'page' | 'full' | ''` in the answer); Notes are only drafted when the request says `options.notes: true` (the page sends it; an older page or app doesn't, so nothing is saved for them), `notes: false` on `save` keeps them out, and a make from a Guide page, more cards from a source, and Live's quiz draft none. The notes are cleaned of links, pictures and HTML (an AI wrote them from material someone else may have written). If the deck is shared, its Guide, notes page included, is public with it. For a language that is set, the options can add Audio cards (off until turned on): words and short phrases with `speak`, a BCP 47 `lang` and what they mean, saved as the app's own audio cards that the device's voice reads. Making cards writes no quiz questions (only Live from a topic does).
- **Files** wait in the person's own private folder under `tmp-` names (`web/blobs.mjs`; a file over 4 MB is sent straight to Supabase Storage with a signed address, a small one through the server), are deleted when the make ends, and a waiting file nobody finished is swept after six hours. A kept file is moved to its kept name and counted in the upload room (Free 500 files / 500 MB, Pro 5,000 / 5 GB).
- **AI**: OpenRouter (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, the key Explain uses). Text (a topic, text from a file, a transcript, Live's quiz) goes to DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, falling back to V4 Flash when no host can answer, and only to the full-precision hosts (fp8 or better, the fastest at writing, since a step must end inside Vercel's 60 seconds); it does not think first unless `LUCIDA_MAKE_REASONING=low` says so (nothing else is allowed), and any `<think>` text in an answer is thrown away. Pictures and scanned pages go to Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite and recordings to Whisper large v3 turbo. Every call asks only for providers that take all of its settings and don't keep or train on what they get. YouTube goes to Gemini directly (`GEMINI_API_KEY`, one public video at a time, ten minutes a part, low picture detail); without the key the page offers pasting the transcript. `LUCIDA_MAKE_MODEL`, `LUCIDA_MAKE_SEE_MODEL`, `LUCIDA_MAKE_STT_MODEL` and `LUCIDA_MAKE_VIDEO_MODEL` change a model. `OPENROUTER_BASE` and `GEMINI_BASE` point at a stand-in for tests. No key is ever in the repo.
- **Limits** are in `web/plans.mjs` (`MAKE`, `MAKE_AUDIO_MB`, `MAKE_CARDS_MAX`, `GUIDE`); the server, the apps, the Pricing page and the privacy policy all read them. Free: 3 makes a day, 30 pages, 15 minutes, 10 pictures, 20 MB a file. Pro: 30 a day, 300 pages, 2 hours, 50 pictures, 40 MB. A make counts when it starts and is given back by Cancel if nothing was written.
- **The deck page** has tabs: Cards, Notes (the Guide and its pages) and Sources (what the cards were made from), with room for one more; Sources is only there for the deck's owner. `?tab=notes` and `?tab=sources` open one. **Sources** (`deck.sources`): what a deck's cards were made from (a file, pictures, a recording, a video, pasted text, a topic, captions), with the text kept for recordings, videos and pasted text so more cards can be made later. They are private, even on a shared deck (the public page only says how many); a card remembers its source and where in it (`card.src`), and the card editor says "Made from ..." and opens the source at that page or time. Deleting a source (or the deck, or your data) deletes its files.
- **Guide** (`deck.guide`, `web/guide.js`): a Markdown page like a README under the deck's header, with extra pages, saved as it is typed, with the last ten versions restorable (kept out of what the app downloads, `GET /api/guide/history`). `guide.js` makes HTML that can't run anything (no raw HTML, `rel="nofollow ugc"`, only the app's own pictures), so the same code draws the editor's preview, the deck page and the public page, and the public page's words for search engines. The AI link has `list_guide_pages`, `get_guide`, `update_guide` and `add_guide_page`.
- **Live from a topic**: Play live can take a topic instead of a deck (`/live/new` with no deck, or the deck's Play live); the maker writes the questions (`options.mode: 'quiz'`, one request) and afterwards Save as a deck keeps them. It counts as a make.

## App Store (the iPhone app)

Run `supabase/appstore.sql` on the database before deploying (it only adds two tables: `blocks` and `apple_purchases`).

- **Pro bought with Apple** (`web/apple.mjs`). The iPhone app buys `cards.lucida.pro.monthly` or `cards.lucida.pro.yearly` with StoreKit 2, buying with the person's id as the appAccountToken (`me.appAccountToken` in `/api/state`), and sends the signed transaction to `POST /api/iap` (`{ signedTransaction }`, or `{ signedTransactions: [...] }` for Restore purchases; answer `{ ok, applied, skipped, plan }`, or `{ error, code }`). Apple's server sends renewals, expiry, refunds, and revocations (App Store Server Notifications V2) to `POST /api/apple`. Neither needs an Apple secret: both are JWS whose certificate chain must lead up to Apple Root CA - G3 (built in, fingerprint pinned), signed for the app `cards.lucida.app`, in the Production or Sandbox App Store. In App Store Connect → the app → App Information → App Store Server Notifications, put `https://app.lucida.cards/api/apple` as both the Production and the Sandbox URL, version 2. Pro from Apple or Stripe both count (the plan is the one that lasts longer), and Settings says "Billed by Apple" for Apple's. `LUCIDA_APPLE_APP_ID` (the app's Apple ID) also checks production notifications, and `LUCIDA_APPLE_SANDBOX=0` refuses Sandbox purchases (they count by default: App Review and TestFlight buy there). On your computer, `LUCIDA_APPLE_TEST_ROOT=<a PEM file>` adds a root of your own so tests can sign purchases themselves; online only Apple's root counts. `LUCIDA_APPLE_TEST_XCODE=1` (on your computer only, never online) also takes purchases signed by Xcode's StoreKit test store, which is what the iPhone app's UI tests buy with (`ios/tools/e2e-store.sh`).
- **Delete account** (`web/account.mjs`, `POST /api/account/delete` with `{ confirm: true }`; Settings → Account). It deletes the library and its files, everything on the study network, blocks, Live rooms, purchase rows, and last the sign-in account itself (Supabase's admin API, with the secret key). A running Stripe subscription is cancelled first, which needs `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` on the server (a restricted key that can write Subscriptions); without it the person is told to cancel Pro first. An Apple subscription can only be cancelled by the person (iPhone Settings → Subscriptions), so the answer says `apple: true` when one is still running.
- **Block** (`web/social.mjs`, the social action `user.block` with `{ handle, on }`, `GET /api/social/blocks`; a profile's ⋯, a suggestion, Settings → Privacy → Blocked people). Someone you blocked can't follow you or suggest to your decks, nothing from them reaches your News, and their decks stay out of your Discover, search, and their profile page.

## Finding decks by school, level, and subject

Run `supabase/school.sql` on the database before deploying (it only adds ten columns and three indexes), and check once online that the school picker finds schools (the server brings `web/schools.json` with it through a literal `import`; if the host leaves it out, picking a school by id says "Pick a school from the list." and "Other" still works).

- **The school list** (`web/schools.json`, made by `node design/schools.mjs hd2024.csv` from the US Department of Education's IPEDS directory, "Institutional Characteristics: Directory information", public domain; download `https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/datacenter/data/HD2024.zip`): the 4,049 open, degree-granting institutions (universities, four-year colleges, community colleges), one row each as `[IPEDS id, name, city, state, other names]` (275 KB, 80 KB zipped). A name two schools share gets its city and state in brackets. **There is no list of high schools** (they may be minors): a high school student picks a level only. `web/school.js` (shared by the server, the web app, and the boards) holds the levels (High school, College, Graduate, Medical or professional, Other), the years (1st to 5th or more), the thirty subjects, and the search (`schoolSearch`: every typed word starts a word of the name, another name, the city, or the state; the iPhone app's `Data/Schools.swift` does the same, and `ios/tests/schools.sh` checks the two agree). The web app fetches the list the first time a picker opens; the iPhone app bundles it (`ios/Lucida/Resources/schools.json`).
- **Your school** (Profile → Edit: Level, School, Year, and "Show my school on my profile"): the school is picked from the list (`schoolId`; its name comes from the list), or typed as "Other" (`school`), or none. **The switch starts off for everyone**, including people who typed a school before: their words stay in the row, hidden. A profile page answers someone else's request without school, level, or year unless the switch is on (a school's own account is an organization, so its name is always shown), and so do the page search engines read and its structured data. Setting a school never makes a profile findable (`listed` turns on only for a handle, bio, subject, or pinned decks), **searching a school finds no people**, and with a level, subject, or school filter search returns decks only. A high school level clears the school (`school_id` and `school`) and refuses one.
- **A public deck's labels** (Deck settings → Sharing, shown for Public decks: Level, Subject, School; `deck.share` takes `level`, `subject`, `schoolId` or typed `school`): the school starts as its owner's the first time the deck goes public, if they show it (not for a high school deck), and the owner can clear it; a school cleared stays cleared (`labeled`). A school that was copied, not picked for the deck (`school_auto`), goes again when its owner stops showing theirs, takes it off, or changes it; one picked for the deck stays. They show in `GET /api/social/mine`, and the deck's tile data carries `level`, `subject`, `school`, and `schoolId`.
- **Discover and search** (`GET /api/public/discover?tag=&level=&subject=&school=`, `/api/public/search?q=&level=&subject=&school=`; `school` is the list's id, or words that find one): with nothing picked, the sections as before and, for someone who set a school (shown or not), "Popular at <school>" first (the public decks labeled with it, ranked like the rest by saves and studies; a school typed as Other matches the same words); with a level, subject, or school (and a topic), one list of decks, best first. The page is Discover's three filter pills (a menu on the web, a sheet on the iPhone) and `/discover?level=college&subject=biology&school=<id>` opens it narrowed. The AI link's `search_shared_decks` takes `level`, `subject`, and `school` (a name like "University of Michigan" or what people call it, "UCLA"), and each deck it finds says its labels.
- The canvas boards: Discover and PhoneDiscover (the filters, with Tweaks for each and for a picker open), WebDiscoverFilters (narrowed, choosing a school), Edit profile (WebProfileEdit, PhoneProfileEdit), and Deck settings → Sharing (WebDeckSettingsShare, PhoneDeckSettingsShare). Tests: `full/school/tests` (school-api, school-ui, school-unit, school-words) and, for the iPhone, `ios/tools/e2e-school.sh`.

## Your data and AI apps

- Decks, cards, and reviews are saved in `data/` on this computer (not in git). Settings → Privacy → Your data exports or deletes them.
- On your computer, AI apps connect over MCP at http://localhost:3000/mcp. For Claude Code: `claude mcp add --transport http lucida http://localhost:3000/mcp`. The Connect AI page sets what an AI may do.
- AI apps can make image cards and audio cards (`web/media.mjs`). A picture can be a link, a file the learner uploaded in ChatGPT (ChatGPT needs Lucida on the internet to reach it), or a file on this computer (for AI apps running here, like Claude Code). For an audio card the AI sends the words and their language. On Pro, Lucida turns them into speech with ElevenLabs or OpenAI when a key is in `.env` (`web/voice.mjs`); on Free, or without a key, the app reads them aloud with the device's voice. A link is looked up once and the connection goes to the address that was checked: nothing on this computer or its network (loopback, private, link-local like 169.254.169.254, shared, multicast and reserved addresses, and IPv4 written inside IPv6) can be fetched, and every way a link can fail says the same plain sentence. Each account has room for so many saved pictures and sounds (Free 500 files and 500 MB, Pro 5,000 files and 5 GB; `LUCIDA_MEDIA_FILES` and `LUCIDA_MEDIA_MB` set it lower for checks); `LUCIDA_TEST_LOCAL_LINKS=1` lets checks fetch from this computer's loopback address.
- Every sound shows its waveform (`web/sound.js`). Recording shows the microphone's level live. A clip's shape is worked out once on the device (from the recording or the uploaded file, or the first time an older clip is shown) and saved with its card, so it's there at once after that; the part already played fills in as it plays, and a tap or a drag on the waveform jumps there. Words read by the device's own voice get a waveform made from the words.
- `node design/snapshot.mjs against <old canvas folder>` lists boards whose look changed, so logic changes can be checked against the canvas.
- On your computer everything is Pro. `LUCIDA_PLAN=free npm run dev` shows the app as it is on Free, to check the Free screens.

## Card formatting

Cards are saved as short markdown (`web/rich.js` reads and writes it; the boards get a copy, so run `node design/build.mjs` after changing it). The editor works like a notes app:

- Start a line with `# `, `## `, or `### ` for a heading, `- ` for a bullet, `1. ` for a numbered list.
- Type `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `~~strike~~` (or `~strike~`), `==highlight==`, or `$math$` and it turns into that style. `[[word]]` makes a blank.
- Type `/` at the start of a line (or after a space) for a menu of headings, lists, a blank, math, an image, or audio. Keep typing to narrow it; ↑ ↓ and Return pick, Esc closes.
- ⌘B bold, ⌘I italic, ⌘U underline, ⌘⇧S strikethrough, ⌘⇧H highlight, ⌘⇧E math, ⌘⌥1–3 headings, ⌘⌥0 plain text, ⌘⌥5 bullets, ⌘⌥6 numbers, ⌘Z undo, ⌘⇧Z redo.
